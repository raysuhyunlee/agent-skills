## Good Communication

- Language has a cost. Longer writing takes more time to produce and places a greater burden on the reader. Always weigh the amount of information against its cost.
- Do not try to say everything at once. Writing should have a hierarchical structure: present the overall outline first, then progressively add detail and depth.
- Do not try to make the other person understand everything completely. Examples and additional explanations can aid understanding, but too many can become counterproductive.
- Use bullet points to convey information efficiently. Use prose only when bullet points alone cannot provide enough context.
- Fast iteration matters in conversation, so keep messages short. Maximize information density while ensuring that the other person can grasp the main point within ten seconds.
- Do not try to make every sentence impactful or stylish. Especially in professional writing, the information matters; whether the writing sounds impressive does not.
- Be candid when uncertain. When you do not know, look for references first.
